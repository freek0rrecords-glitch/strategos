                OLI Paradigm (Eclectic)

                Dunning's theory: three advantages must exist for FDI to be optimal over exporting or licensing.

                O — Ownership Advantages

                Firm-specific assets giving you an edge in foreign markets: technology, brand, management skills, scale.

                L — Location Advantages

                Host-country attributes making it attractive for investment: labour, resources, market size, regulations.

                I — Internalisation Advantages

                Why internalise rather than license: protect IP, control quality, avoid opportunism.

                FDI Decision Matrix

                O + L + I = All Present
                → Foreign Direct Investment
                O + I present · L absent
                → Exporting
                O + L present · I absent
                → Licensing / JV

                ACADEMIC CONTEXT & LIMITATIONS

                The CAGE framework was developed by Pankaj Ghemawat (IESE/Harvard) and published in Harvard Business Review (2001) and Redefining Global Strategy (2007). It is widely used in international strategy consulting and MBA programmes.

                Limitations to note: CAGE is a structured qualitative framework, not a predictive model. Scores reflect the analyst's judgement and available data. The framework does not capture all sources of friction (e.g. competitive dynamics, technological readiness) and should be used alongside Porter's Five Forces, OLI Paradigm and Uppsala Model for a complete internationalisation picture.

                Gravity model supplement: For quantitative validation, use the gravity model (Trade = k × GDP_A × GDP_B / Distance²) to benchmark actual vs theoretical trade volumes. Significant underperformance of actual trade vs model suggests higher effective distance than CAGE scores alone imply.

                Uppsala Internationalisation Model

                Johanson & Vahlne's stage-by-stage model of gradual foreign market commitment.

                Stage 1 — No Regular Exports

                Purely domestic operations. No systematic export activity.

                Stage 2 — Export via Agents

                Irregular export through independent representatives.

                Stage 3 — Sales Subsidiaries

                Establish sales offices abroad. Higher commitment, more market knowledge.

                Stage 4 — Production Abroad

                Full local production. Maximum involvement and knowledge.

                Psychic Distance Sequencing

                Uppsala proposes firms first enter markets that are "psychically close" — similar in language, culture and institutions — before moving further.

                Leontief Paradox & Limitations

                Leontief (1953) found the USA exported more labour-intensive goods despite being capital-abundant — contradicting H-O. Modern extensions: Human Capital (Kenen), Technology Gaps (Posner), Product Life Cycle (Vernon). Use H-O as a starting hypothesis, then refine.
